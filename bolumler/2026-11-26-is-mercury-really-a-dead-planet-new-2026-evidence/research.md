## Key facts
- BepiColombo is a joint ESA/JAXA mission, launched 20 Oct 2018 on Ariane 5 (VA245) from Kourou. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BepiColombo]
- It is the first European and Japanese mission to Mercury, the first two-orbiter mission there, and only the second mission ever to orbit Mercury (after NASA's MESSENGER). [https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/BepiColombo/BepiColombo_arrival_media_kit]
- Orbit arrival date VERIFIED, with a caveat. ESA's 23 Nov 2026 update: "On 21 November, starting at 18:24 UTC, BepiColombo entered orbit around Mercury." [https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/BepiColombo/Latest_updates_BepiColombo_s_arrival_at_Mercury]
  - The brake burn began at 18:24 UTC at about 635 km altitude. Contact was lost during the burn until 21:37 UTC. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BepiColombo]
  - A software glitch put the stack into safe mode and ended the burn early. It got about 77% of the planned velocity change (Wikipedia figure, one source). The result is a 75-hour orbit instead of the planned 58-hour one. ESA says no extra thruster burns are needed. [ESA latest updates; Wikipedia]
  - The "21 November" claim is therefore correct. It is a capture into a polar orbit of the still-stacked MPO + Mio + sunshield, NOT the final science orbit. Avoid saying "BepiColombo is now studying Mercury from its final orbit".
- Next milestones (as of ESA 23 Nov): Mio separates from MPO on 9-10 Dec 2026. The MOSIF sunshield is released 16 Dec. MPO reaches its final orbit around 10 Mar 2027. Science phase starts April 2027. ESA says dates may change. [ESA latest updates; search summary of ESA timeline https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2018/10/BepiColombo_arrival_at_Mercury_timeline]
- Hardware: MPO (11 instruments, incl. BELA laser altimeter, MERMAG, MERTIS, MGNS, MIXS, SERENA, SIMBIO-SYS, PHEBUS) and JAXA's Mio (MPPE, magnetometer, PWI, MSASI sodium imager, dust monitor). Cost estimate about 1.65 billion euros (2017 figure). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BepiColombo]
- Six Mercury flybys: 1 Oct 2021, 23 Jun 2022, 19 Jun 2023, 4 Sep 2024, 1 Dec 2024, 8 Jan 2025. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BepiColombo]
- ESA frames Mercury's open puzzles: an inner core that seems too large, surprising chemistry, water ice despite surface temperatures up to 450 C. [ESA media kit above]

## Latest developments (with dates)
- 21 Nov 2026: BepiColombo enters Mercury orbit, with safe mode and a short burn (see above). 23 Nov: ESA says the mission is "safe and on track" for the 9-10 Dec separation. [ESA latest updates]
- 3 Sep 2026: Transfer module (MTM) separation, the start of the arrival phase. 6 Oct 2026: first-light instrument tests (BELA, MERTIS, SIMBIO-SYS). [ESA latest updates]
- 10 Sep 2026: Shrinking. Nishiyama and colleagues (DLR Institute of Space Research), Geophysical Research Letters, "Underestimation of Planetary Contraction Due To Obscuration by Surface Roughness: The Case of Mercury". Upper estimate of total diameter loss is about 23 km, versus an earlier 4-16 km range, i.e. roughly 10-30% more contraction. Impact debris in rough terrain is argued to hide thrust-fault structures. Based on MESSENGER data (resolution roughly 5 km features). [snippet-level: search summaries of connectsci.au, Scientific American, Fox, Universe Today. Space.com apparently quotes ~19 km in one passage, so the number is inconsistent between outlets. Check the paper.]
- 27 Jan 2026: Bright streaks. Bickel et al. (University of Bern), Communications Earth & Environment, "Slope Lineae as Potential Indicators of Recent Volatile Loss on Mercury". Machine learning applied to about 112,000 MESSENGER images found about 400 (402) slope lineae, 93% in the north because of image coverage. They are on sun-facing slopes of young craters and often start at hollows. Interpretation: ongoing or recent outgassing of volatiles (e.g. sulfur or other light elements). About 10% are not in craters, so other mechanisms may act. [snippet-level: https://mediarelations.unibe.ch/media_releases/2026/media_releases_2026/streaks_mercury/index_eng.html ; https://eos.org/articles/oozing-gas-could-be-making-stripes-in-mercurys-craters ; EGU abstract https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-12154.html]
- Sep 2026 (EPSC2026 abstracts, unreviewed conference material): possible ancient buried pyroclastic deposits near Glinka crater; multiple explosive episodes at Agwo and Abeeso faculae; a tentative huge scoria cone in Caloris. [https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EPSC2026/EPSC2026-486.html and related abstracts; snippet-level]
- A 2026 Planetary Science Journal paper is titled "Spectral Evidence for Recent/Ongoing Activity in Mercury's Praxiteles Basin". Only the title was seen. UNVERIFIED content.
- 2025 (JGR Planets): Deutsch, Bickel, Blewett: most hollows are in an early stage and likely still evolving. They say BepiColombo can look for change. [https://eos.org/editor-highlights/mercurys-hollows-may-be-young-and-active]

## Competing hypotheses / open questions
- Is the "dead planet" label wrong? "Not geologically dead" is supported by indirect evidence only: landforms, spectra and counts, not an observed event. No eruption, new fault scarp or new streak has been observed on Mercury. Present this as "probably still active", not "proven".
- Streak origin: volatile outgassing (Bern team) versus other mechanisms. About 10% of lineae lie outside craters. An outside scientist found the link to volatiles plausible. [Eos snippet]
- Hollows: formed by volatile loss (sublimation) from sulfide- or halide-bearing rock. NASA says they are about 100,000 years old on average and may still be forming. [https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mercury/mercurys-strange-hollows]
- Shrinking: the total amount (4-16 km vs up to 23 km) depends on mapping method. Whether faults are active today is debated. The 2023 Nature Geoscience study (Man et al., Open University) counted 727 small grabens (190 "certain"), estimated at 300 million years old or younger, and read them as sign of ongoing contraction. The authors say this may be refuted by BepiColombo imagery. [https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-023-01281-5 ; https://www.open.ac.uk/blogs/news/science-mct/mercury-shrinking-planet-is-still-getting-smaller-new-research ; snippet-level]
- Volcanism: ancient effusive and explosive volcanism is established. Whether any vent is active today is unconfirmed. Carbon-rich magma (Reitze et al.) is contested, since measured crustal carbon is low. [Universe Today / Eos snippets]
- Heat engine: Mercury has a global magnetic field and a large metal core that is partly molten (general knowledge, not sourced here). Why this is so remains an open question. BepiColombo's MPO/Mio magnetometers and gravity (MORE) data target this.

## Numbers & scale comparisons (make big numbers intuitive)
- 23 km of diameter loss is about 2.5 times the height of Mount Everest (8.85 km, general knowledge), shaved off a planet about 4,880 km across (general knowledge). About 0.5% of the diameter.
- Journey: launch 20 Oct 2018 to orbit capture 21 Nov 2026 is about 8 years 1 month. Planned 9 flybys: 1 Earth, 2 Venus, 6 Mercury.
- Orbit: planned 58 h, actual 75 h (about 3 days around the planet per lap). 635 km altitude at burn start (similar to the ISS-to-Sun-synchronous range, roughly 1.5 times ISS height; ISS about 400 km, general knowledge).
- About 400 streaks from about 112,000 images, so roughly one streak per 280 images.
- Brake burn: planned 37 minutes, ended early with about 77% of planned delta-v.
- Hollows about 100,000 years old on average, fresh by geological standards (Mercury about 4.5 billion years old, general knowledge). That is about 0.002% of the planet's age.
- Cost about 1.65 billion euros (2017 estimate). [Wikipedia]

## Common misconceptions to avoid
- "BepiColombo is already in its final science orbit." It is not. The final orbit is around 10 Mar 2027 and science starts April 2027.
- "The 21 Nov insertion went perfectly." It did not. There was a software glitch, safe mode and a shorter burn. It was recovered with an adapted plan. Do not overdramatise it either: ESA calls it safe and on track.
- "BepiColombo has found new activity." No. All 2026 evidence still comes from MESSENGER data (2011-2015) and earlier. BepiColombo has no new results on streaks, shrinkage or volcanism yet.
- "Mercury is erupting / a volcano was seen." No eruption has been observed. Claims about recent volcanism are inferences from deposits and spectra, and mostly conference abstracts.
- "Streaks are water or lava flows." The proposed cause is gas and volatile loss, and it is not proven.
- "Mercury is shrinking by 23 km radius." The figure is about the diameter (check the paper), and outlets disagree on 19 vs 23 km.
- "Mercury is the hottest planet." No, Venus is hotter (general knowledge). Mercury has extreme day-night swings, and ice sits in permanently shadowed polar craters.
- "Mercury was never visited before." MESSENGER orbited it 2011-2015 and Mariner 10 flew by in 1974-75 (general knowledge, not sourced here).
