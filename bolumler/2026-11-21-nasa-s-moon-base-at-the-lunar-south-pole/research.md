## Key facts
- NASA's "Moon Base" is billed as humanity's first lunar outpost near the lunar South Pole, built in three phases through crewed and uncrewed missions. [https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase/]
- Phase One (now to 2029) is "Gain Reliable Access, Experiment, and Learn": robotic missions, tech demos and site scouting. Phase Two (2029-2032) is "Build and Expand": early power, cargo, logistics and communications. Phase Three (2032 onward) is "Live and Work on the Moon": a permanent outpost with habitats, power, comms and transport. [https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/]
- The pivot was announced in March 2026. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman paused the Gateway lunar-orbit station indefinitely to focus on a surface base. Cost is reported as about $20 billion. That figure comes from press reports, not an audited NASA budget. NASA says Gateway is not cancelled, but its hardware may be repurposed. [https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/3/24/nasa-to-spend-20bn-on-moon-base-nuclear-powered-mars-spacecraft] [https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/24/goodbye_lunar_gateway_nasa_ditches/]
- Moon Base missions are numbered. Moon Base I is Blue Origin's Blue Moon Mark 1 "Endurance", landing at the Shackleton Connecting Ridge. Moon Base II is Astrobotic's Griffin, with over 1,100 pounds of cargo including Astrolab's FLIP rover. Moon Base III is Intuitive Machines' Nova-C "Trinity" with Lunar Vertex. [https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-moon-base-rovers-landers-missions/]
- Phase One hardware. PROMISE is a six-wheeled nuclear-powered rover with a roughly 1 m drill and an 18-month mission. VIPER is slated for late 2027 on a second Blue Moon MK1. MoonFall is four hopping drones from JPL in 2028 to map landing sites. Two crewed-capable unpressurised rovers (Astrolab CLV-1, Lunar Outpost Pegasus) are due by 2028. CAPSTONE 02 is planned for 2027. [https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/]
- Phase Two habitat: a JAXA Pressurized Rover, a mobile lab where crews work without spacesuits. Missions of up to 28 days. [https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/]
- Why the south pole: permanently shadowed regions (PSRs) hold water ice; the huge South Pole-Aitken Basin is nearby; the pole lies in Shackleton Crater. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole] [https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase/]
- Power: NASA targets a roughly 100 kW fission reactor on the surface by around 2030 (Fission Surface Power). Experts doubt the timeline, not so much the size. [https://www.aol.com/news/experts-sound-off-trump-nasa-131201551.html] (secondary, via search summary)

## Competing hypotheses / open questions
- Can the schedule hold? Experts doubt the 2027-2028 landing dates and the 2030 reactor. The Artemis lander still needs development. [https://www.aol.com/news/experts-sound-off-trump-nasa-131201551.html]
- Race with China: it plans a crewed landing "before 2030" or "by 2030" and the International Lunar Research Station (with Russia), with construction from about 2031 per Scientific American. Dates are not firm. [https://scientificamerican.com/article/china-delays-ambitious-change-7-moon-mission-until-2027] [https://www.futura-sciences.com/en/?p=38171]
- How much ice is there, and in what form? Over 600 billion kg is a low-end radar estimate, and the ice's origin (comets, asteroids, solar wind, Earth) is unsettled. Chandrayaan-3's ChaSTE measured high-latitude temperatures but did not directly detect ice. [https://www.planetary.org/articles/water-on-the-moon-guide] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole]
- Fate of Gateway and the partners (ESA, Japan, Canada). [https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/25/nasa_gateway_opinion/]
- Is $20B realistic for a permanent base? Press figure only.
- Real access to PSRs: dust, slopes, and extreme cold make a landing and mining hard. NASA notes steep slopes, ridges and deep craters. [https://www.nasa.gov/mission/promise/]

## Numbers & scale comparisons
- More than 600 billion kg of polar ice, about 240,000 Olympic pools. [https://www.planetary.org/articles/water-on-the-moon-guide]
- PSRs reach about -250 C, colder than Pluto. Sunlit rims about -50 C. Daytime surface about 120 C. [same]
- LCROSS 2009 detected about 155 kg of water: a few bathtubs from an impact. [same]
- One lunar day is about 14 Earth days of sunlight, then 14 of night. [https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/] (MoonFall drones fly across a ~14-day day)
- 100 kW reactor: roughly an office building's power, per an expert quoted in coverage. 20 kW test reactor on SR-1 Freedom. [https://www.aol.com/news/experts-sound-off-trump-nasa-131201551.html]
- $20B over about seven years, roughly $2.9B a year (my arithmetic). Seven years is from CBS via search summary. [https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nasa-moon-base-plan-lunar-south-pole/]
- Each LTV contract about $220M. Rover about 2,000 lb (Astrolab).

## Common misconceptions to avoid
- "Base" does not mean a finished city now. Permanent habitats are Phase Three, 2032 and beyond. Today it is robotic landers and demos.
- Gateway was paused, not formally cancelled.
- Artemis II did not land. Artemis III's role is disputed or reportedly changed.
- Moon water is not lakes. It is ice mixed into regolith, and the amounts are uncertain.
- "Sunless pole" is wrong. Some ridges get near-continuous sunlight, and the PSRs are separate.
- The $20B is a press figure.
- Chandrayaan-3 did not find ice directly.
- The pole is not the same as the far side; South Pole-Aitken is partly far side.
